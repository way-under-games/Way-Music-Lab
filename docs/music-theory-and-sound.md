Research and implementation notes for Way Music, October 4, 2026. The user's target combines fantasy orchestral color with math-rock rhythm and variable time signatures. Our recommendation is to improve composition, performance, and instruments as separate layers, keeping the musical plan portable across browser and native game engines.

## Findings from the current generator

Version 0.2 had a shared scale, chord progression, seeded motif, and additive meter map. Its main composition limitations were root-position accompaniment that moved with every chord, melody figures transposed by chord degree, little distinction between stable and decorative notes, and limited phrase development. Every short-note instrument used essentially the same performance on repeated notes. These are observations from `src/composer.mjs` and `src/webaudio.mjs`, rather than findings about procedural music in general.

Before the 0.4 instrument update, the renderer approximated instruments with filtered oscillators or synthesized percussion. Its simple sample loader only chose the nearest root and changed playback rate. Version 0.4 now bundles 95 CC0 VSCO CE recordings with pitch zones, two velocity layers, deterministic alternate takes, short/sustain articulation selection, prepared sustain loops, and a shared algorithmically generated room impulse. Live playback and WAV export use the same bank and event-to-take selection. Separate release recordings, continuous dynamic crossfades, legato, and measured native parity remain unimplemented.

## Turning theory into executable choices

These are style heuristics for our current tonal soundtrack. They are not universal definitions of musical quality, and they should eventually be selectable by style.

| Concept and evidence | Our algorithm | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voice leading connects individual lines. Common tones can stay in place while other voices move to nearby chord tones; classical exercises also constrain spacing, crossing, and parallel perfect intervals. [Roger Williams University textbook](https://rwu.pressbooks.pub/musictheory/chapter/root-position-connection-of-chords-in-the-major-mode/) | Enumerate three-voice inversions in a bounded register. Minimize semitone movement, penalize larger leaps and moved common tones, keep voice order and spacing, and apply a soft parallel-fifth/octave penalty. Keep bass below the chord. | Implemented in `voiceTriad`. It does not implement complete SATB counterpoint. |
| Tonal melodic tendencies include proximity, a clear contour, and movement toward an ending. Strict cantus-firmus constraints are specific to that exercise. [Open Music Theory](https://openmusictheory.github.io/cantusFirmus.html) | Choose melodic anchors from chord tones, use a bounded register and movement cost, and aim the closing melody at the tonic. | Implemented. Cross-bar melodic movement still needs more planning. |
| Passing tones connect stable tones by step; neighbor tones step away and return. [Open Music Theory on embellishment](https://open-musictheory.github.io/docs/voice-leading-and-model-composition/strict-four-voice-composition/embellishingTones/) | Add a short weak-position ornament only when it can connect by one or two semitones to both adjacent stable notes. Keep it quieter than the anchors. | Implemented. Suspensions and anticipations are deferred. |
| Sentence form presents a repeated basic idea and develops it toward a cadence through techniques such as fragmentation and acceleration. [Open Music Theory on sentences](https://openmusictheory.github.io/sentence.html) | Plan an eight-bar phrase before emitting events. Repeat a two-bar pitch-contour idea, modify targets during continuation, then simplify and leave a closing breath. | Implemented as a simplified phrase heuristic, not a faithful classical sentence analysis. |
| Harmonic minor raises the seventh, creating a semitone leading into the tonic. [Open Music Theory on minor scales](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/minor-scales/) | In minor, use the raised seventh over the major dominant V and return to natural minor elsewhere. Favor leading-tone resolution upward at V–i; let Tension end on V to remain unresolved. | Implemented; no arbitrary chromatic notes or automatic modulation. |
| Compound meter organizes subdivisions into larger beats, and the grouping matters to perceived accents. [Open Music Theory on compound meter](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/compound-meters-and-time-signatures/) | Retain the existing explicit accent map, including 3+3 for 6/8 and unequal groups such as 2+2+3 for 7/8. Fit phrase targets to group starts rather than assuming four quarter-note beats. | Implemented. |

The theory module is `src/theory.mjs`. The composer reconstructs all eight phrase frames even when a host requests one bar, preserving deterministic seeking and matching full/partial generation. No renderer API or game engine is required to apply these rules. These foundations were introduced in generator 0.3.0; generator 0.5.0 adds authored motif and narrative arrangements, while score schema remains version 2. Old serialized note events remain playable; retain the generator version or actual score for precise replay. See `docs/narrative-scoring.md` for the newer director, progression and remaining transition work.

## Rhythm beyond changing signatures

Our proposed next math-rock work is an authored riff vocabulary with rhythmic motifs that recur across changing bar lengths. Transform a recognizable motif by rotation, one subdivision added or removed, or a controlled displacement; alternate dense passages with space. Synchronize deliberate ensemble accents while letting one part create tension against them. This is a design proposal, not a claim that odd time signatures alone define math rock.

Keep separate controls for meter, grouping, onset pattern, density, and phrase length. A 7/8 bar with 2+2+3 accents can contain several very different riffs. Add proper ties across beats/bars before claiming full syncopation or suspensions: current score validation confines notes to a bar. True polyrhythm needs independent subdivision grids; polymeter needs independent part boundaries. Neither follows automatically from alternating time signatures. Preserve exact ticks and an intentional landing point when adding either.

An eight-bar phrase in changing meters varies in elapsed duration. A later planner should support phrase lengths measured in pulses or musical sections as well as bars. Game intensity should choose development, register, instrumentation, and rhythmic pressure; simply increasing note count tends to obscure the theme. Abrupt scene/key changes still need transition phrases or compatible boundary voicings.

## Improving instrument sound

The first orchestral bank now uses a curated subset of **VSCO 2 Community Edition**. Its publisher explicitly provides CC0 WAV samples and SFZ mappings. Our pinned-source importer downmixes and resamples the selected recordings to 24 kHz mono PCM, trims tails, adds edge fades, and prepares crossfaded sustain loops. Source and derivative hashes, numeric MIDI roots from the upstream maps, and credits are retained in the manifest. This is a subset and preprocessing of that library, not newly recorded instruments. [Versilian Studios](https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/)

The **Philharmonia** library is another useful source of orchestral recordings, including plucked instruments and percussion. Its site says the samples are free to use. Inspect the chosen download's accompanying terms and metadata when building the bank; retain source and provenance rather than assuming every free library has identical packaging rights. [Philharmonia sound samples](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/sound-samples/)

Use a portable instrument manifest with note zones, recorded root, velocity bounds, articulation, alternate takes, gain, tuning, loop points, release behavior, and source/license metadata. Separate expressive score controls from instrument-specific file paths. For repeated plucks and drum hits, alternate recorded takes deterministically; select a recorded dynamic layer before applying gain. The SFZ tutorials explain velocity layers, round-robin sequencing, and one-shot drum playback. [SFZ drum tutorial](https://sfzformat.com/tutorials/drum_basics/)

For a native sampler, evaluate **sfizz** behind the renderer contract rather than writing a complete SFZ engine. The project provides an SFZ synthesizer and is a reuse candidate; browser/native parity, build footprint, packaging, and the selected license obligations still need an actual integration proof. [SFZTools](https://sfz.tools/sfizz/)

Our proposed playback sequence is: select articulation and dynamic layer; select a nearby pitch zone and alternate take; schedule an instrument-appropriate envelope; apply track balance and room send; retain master headroom. Add one shared room with restrained wet level instead of independent long reverbs on every note. Web Audio provides convolution and custom periodic-wave primitives if we need those browser effects or better fallback synthesis. [Web Audio specification](https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/)

Random timing jitter is not the first remedy. Keep ensemble accents together, then consider bounded, repeatable timing and duration deviations for selected parts. Phrase-shaped dynamics and articulation choices should come before arbitrary randomness. Sustain samples loop so a held score note can outlast its recording. Offline exports now use the same bank/settings as live playback for meaningful comparisons.

## Development order and listening gates

1. **Composition foundation, completed in this pass:** smooth accompaniment, harmonic targets, controlled ornaments, motif development, cadences, and phrase dynamics. Add source-linked tests for properties rather than snapshots of every note.
2. **Recorded-instrument foundation, implemented in 0.4:** 95 CC0 recordings for violin/cello ensemble, harp, flute, horn, and percussion; zones/layers/alternate takes and short/sustain selection; matching live/offline playback. The bundled WAV payload is 9,666,618 bytes; decoded memory depends on the device sample rate. Listener preference and longer sessions under game load still need evaluation.
3. **Theme and riff authoring:** editable melodic/rhythmic motifs, phrase vocabulary, richer chord colors, and constrained choices weighted by style. Preserve explicit reasons for each choice and deterministic seeds.
4. **Transitions and expressive performance:** shared-tone scene transitions, phrase quantization, ties/suspensions, articulation changes, and calibrated dynamics. Then test native/browser instrument parity through a real adapter.

Audition composition with the same renderer and loudness first; audition instrument changes with the same score second. Use several seeds, scenes, energy levels, and meter cycles. Ask listeners whether they recognize the motif, hear a clear direction/ending, feel the meter, and find the density appropriate. Blind preference checks are more useful than treating consonance or fewer leaps as an objective quality score. Compare transitions under game load as well as standalone renders.

`scripts/inspect-score.mjs` reports accompaniment motion and melodic interval statistics to locate problems. These are structural measurements, not proof that a track sounds good. `VERIFICATION.md` records test and render results. Longer-term thematic development, cross-scene continuity, full orchestral expression, and Unreal integration remain incomplete. Recorded sample playback is implemented; measured technical checks do not establish studio-quality realism.
