# Memorable game scores and composition research for Way

Research date: October 5, 2026. Original analysis and proposed exercises: MIT. This study concerns composition and interactive musical form; it adds no software dependencies or soundtrack assets.

The working hypothesis for Way is that a score becomes memorable when players learn a distinctive musical identity and encounter it again under changing circumstances. Better instrument recordings help communicate that identity, but they cannot supply it. Our next research priority is to author a theme with a recognizable rhythm, develop it across the game's dramatic arc, and test its identity and emotional effect with listeners.

## Evidence and selection

There is no single defensible worldwide ranking of game scores in the sources reviewed here. Streams, listener polls, awards, concert programming, and game sales measure different things. Sales are particularly poor evidence that a soundtrack itself is popular. This is a comparative study of twelve score traditions, including the four references requested for Way, rather than a numbered popularity chart.

Two unusually strong signs of lasting cultural recognition are the *Super Mario Bros.* Ground Theme's 2023 National Recording Registry induction, discussed in [Koji Kondo's Library of Congress interview](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/koji-kondo-interview.pdf), and *Minecraft: Volume Alpha*'s [2025 induction](https://www.loc.gov/item/prn-25-024/national-recording-registry-inducts-sounds-of-elton-john-chicago-broadways-hamilton-mary-j-blige-amy-winehouse-minecraft-and-microsoft/2025-04-09/). These recognize cultural, historical, or aesthetic importance, not comparative listening totals.

An audience preference signal comes from [Classic FM's own account of its polls](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/periods-genres/video-game/): Final Fantasy reached number 17 in 2016 and Kingdom Hearts number 86 in 2021. Those are dated results from a particular radio audience; the Final Fantasy entry represents franchise music, not a proof that *VII* is the world's most popular score. The remaining cases provide contrasting composition and implementation approaches, not independently measured positions in a popularity ranking.

Each case below distinguishes a composer's documented account from a proposed Way lesson. The interviews support intent and process; they do not experimentally establish the cause of a score's memorability. This research pass reviewed written material, not the complete audio of every soundtrack. Track comparisons later in the document are listening assignments, not completed transcriptions.

## What music memory research can establish

Jakubowski, Finkel, Stewart, and Müllensiefen's *Dissecting an Earworm* used reports from 3,000 survey participants and compared 100 frequently named tunes with 100 matched tunes. Its [author repository abstract](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1371466/dissecting-an-earworm-melodic-features-and-song-popularity-predict-involuntary-musical-imagery) reports associations with chart success and recency, more common overall melodic contours, less common gradients between melodic turning points, and faster average tempi. The article's DOI is [10.1037/aca0000090](https://doi.org/10.1037/aca0000090); the abstract was available in the search index, while the full repository PDF could not be retrieved in this pass.

These are findings about involuntary musical imagery in a pop-song corpus. They do not prove that making Way faster, adding a leap, or maximizing a numerical contour score will produce a beloved soundtrack. They also do not measure narrative identification or player immersion. A useful research question is whether an understandable shape with a distinctive detail remains recognizable across our arrangements. Familiarity and exposure must be measured alongside the composition itself.

## Comparative score studies

### Super Mario Bros and Koji Kondo

**Documented account:** Kondo describes engaging rhythm, memorable melody, and a small number of sound channels. He explains the Ground Theme's interaction between even subdivisions in melody/bass and triple subdivisions in the hi-hat, and its four distinct motifs. He also acknowledges the effect of decades of repeated play and appearances outside games. [Library of Congress interview, April 12, 2023](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/koji-kondo-interview.pdf).

**Way lesson to test:** Recognition can live in rhythm and phrase contrast as much as pitch. Try a compact theme with an identifiable rhythmic opening and a contrasting answer. Ask whether it survives a plain instrument and an unfamiliar arrangement. Hardware limitations encouraged economy here; they are not a requirement for modern memorable music.

### The Legend of Zelda Ocarina of Time and Koji Kondo

**Documented account:** Kondo describes Hyrule Field as interchangeable eight-bar components, including combat material. He designed their endings to connect harmonically and avoided repeatedly restarting the same music when the player returned from a dungeon. Enemy proximity could lead into combat material without a disruptive wholesale change. [Nintendo's Iwata Asks sound interview, section 2, Japanese original](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/3ds/interview/aqej/vol1/index2.html).

**Way lesson to test:** Procedural variety works better when the composer designs the joints. Explore several authored continuations with compatible entry and exit harmony. Random ordering alone is not a musical transition policy. This interview provides a concrete historical design, not proof of a universal first implementation.

### Final Fantasy and Nobuo Uematsu

**Documented account:** Uematsu describes deriving character music from scripts and artwork, names Aeris's theme from *VII* as a favorite, and explains that *X*'s *To Zanarkand* began as another commission before its opening-scene use. He emphasizes individuality and distinguishes film's fixed timing from game loops. [Anime Boston Q&A, April 2010, transcribed and translated by the credited interview team](https://squareenixmusic.com/features/interviews/nobuouematsu2.shtml).

**Way lesson to test:** Give the theme a character or dramatic relationship before assigning generic mood labels. Study how the same music feels before and after a narrative event. A melody can be memorable without having been composed from an elaborate technical system; theory should help develop a voice rather than standardize it.

### Halo and Martin O'Donnell with Michael Salvatori

**Documented account:** O'Donnell's GDC paper describes combining ancient/mysterious chant with strings and rock rhythm, composing material linearly before arranging it for interaction, and deliberately limiting musical coverage. Its implementation uses entrance, loop, alternate, and exit material with different transition behaviors. [O'Donnell's March 24, 2002 paper, hosted by halo.bungie.org](https://halo.bungie.org/misc/gdc.2002.music/).

**Way lesson to test:** A clear timbral identity and selective entrances may make a return more noticeable. Reserve some orchestration for consequential moments. Distinguish a soft fade from a composed ending, and evaluate whether continuous accompaniment weakens the dramatic effect. Silence needs an authored policy.

### Minecraft and C418

**Documented account:** Daniel Rosenfeld describes wanting organic, piano-centered beauty in contrast with the blocky world, and a melancholy response to its early solitude. He explicitly requested silence to avoid tiring players. He reports players attaching emotion to music entering during ordinary activities. This is his account of the original implementation, not a specification for all current editions. [C418 interview transcript, Twenty Thousand Hertz, April 14, 2021](https://www.20k.org/episodes/minecraft).

**Way lesson to test:** Music can leave room for the player to create meaning rather than explain every action. Compare reactive exploration music with sparse, less prescriptive entrances. Record whether listeners feel accompanied, instructed, or crowded. An unscored passage can prepare an important musical return.

### Undertale and Toby Fox

**Documented account:** Fox describes learning favorite game melodies and chords by ear, paying attention to the emotions of progressions, and often having music ready before assembling a scene so the scene can match it. Some important *Undertale* tracks existed beforehand. [Fox and Yoko Shimomura's official Square Enix conversation, May 19, 2023](https://www.square-enix-games.com/en_US/news/live-live-yoko-shimomura-toby-fox-pt-2).

**Way lesson to test:** Prototype a scene and its theme together. Let an effective phrase influence the dramatic timing. The proposed listening study should trace returns and transformations of themes; this source does not itself provide a note-by-note leitmotif map. Fox's [own rejected-track discussion for Deltarune](https://toby.fangamer.com/interviews/chapter2_music/) also demonstrates that a usable composition can still be wrong for a scene's identity.

### Celeste and Lena Raine

**Documented account:** Raine wanted a reusable identifying melody, described its contour as a climb, and developed the Celestial Resort music around progression and Mr. Oshiro's increasing instability. Her involvement in prototyping let music and level structure inform one another. [Written interview highlights, Gaming Trend, February 15, 2018](https://gamingtrend.com/podcasts/gaming-trend-podcast-behind-the-music-of-celeste-lena-raine-interview/).

**Way lesson to test:** Gameplay progress does not always mean a happier arrangement. Completing a task might expose a character's distress. Design changes around what the action means in the story, then choose rhythm, harmony, and density accordingly. A rising contour is one composer's expressive choice, not a universal representation of climbing.

### DOOM 2016 and Mick Gordon

**Documented scope:** Gordon's GDC session description frames the score as aggressive, prominent, and supportive of gameplay, combining composition, synthesis, musical sound design, interactive music, and mixing. Only the session description was reviewed here, not the complete presentation. [DOOM Behind the Music, GDC 2017](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024068/).

**Way lesson to test:** A memorable identity may reside in attack, pulse, bass movement, and texture as well as a singable tune. Study those dimensions separately during listening. Increasing volume or adding orchestral layers is not automatically a stronger battle score; the rhythm and mix must preserve meaningful game sounds.

### Journey and Austin Wintory

**Documented account:** Wintory describes an evolving single-theme score, cello representing the player, and other instruments representing the world. Musical triggering was iterated in the game. He also distinguishes the album arrangement from an unedited gameplay capture. [Firsthand interview, TheSixthAxis, March 15, 2012](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2012/03/15/interview-journey-composer-austin-wintory/).

**Way lesson to test:** Maintain one identity across vulnerability, motion, danger, and arrival. Change its surroundings and development rather than replace it with unrelated presets. Evaluate the live score within gameplay; a convincing album excerpt does not prove that transitions work when players linger or change direction.

### Everybody's Gone to the Rapture and Jessica Curry

**Documented account:** Curry describes character-specific musical arcs, scene-specific linear cues, travelling material that can be divided horizontally and instrumentally, and procedural music. She connects the pastoral sound and human voices with absence, memory, and human connection. Ghost audio integrates music with the surrounding sound world. [Andy Price's firsthand interview, NME, May 4, 2022](https://www.nme.com/features/gaming-features/jessica-curry-on-soundtracking-everybodys-gone-to-the-raptures-poignant-exploration-of-loss-3218264).

**Way lesson to test:** Distinguish location, character, story, and momentary activity. They need not share one intensity control. Preserve important authored scenes while allowing exploratory passages to unfold variably. Our fixed identity per game remains a deliberate Way choice; we need not adopt every aspect of this game's procedural approach.

### The Last of Us and Gustavo Santaolalla

**Documented account:** Naughty Dog describes an intimate score tied to narrative and characters' perceptions. [Eric Monacelli, Naughty Dog, PlayStation Blog, May 17, 2013](https://blog.playstation.com/2013/05/17/the-music-of-the-last-of-us-gustavo-santaolalla/).

**Way lesson to test:** Emotional weight can come from an exposed line and space around it. Compare a sparse statement of our theme with a full arrangement at equal perceived loudness. Study attack, decay, silence, and the distance between phrases. This article alone does not establish a transcription or the exact instrumental setup of every track.

### Silent Hill 2 and Akira Yamaoka

**Documented account:** Yamaoka describes scoring loneliness, sadness, anxiety, and psychological fear rather than simply writing frightening music. He discusses using a broad sound palette and silence in service of narrative. [Andy Price's firsthand interview, MusicRadar, July 1, 2026](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/rather-than-simply-writing-scary-music-i-wanted-to-express-loneliness-sadness-anxiety-and-the-psychological-aspects-of-fear-we-speak-to-video-game-horror-soundtrack-godfather-akira-yamaoka-about-the-legacy-of-silent-hill).

**Way lesson to test:** Beauty and unease can coexist. Avoid treating danger as the only negative state or loss as simply a slower battle track. Test restrained dissonance, an unresolved ending, or a changed timbre while retaining a familiar theme. These are proposed experiments, not verified analyses of individual *Silent Hill 2* chord progressions.

## Music theory questions that matter for an interactive score

The following is our proposed composition framework. It uses general musical concepts, not borrowed score transcriptions or a claim that these twelve games implement the same rules.

### Theme identity and development

A theme needs more than a list of scale degrees. Specify its intervals and contour, onset rhythm, note lengths, rests, accents, phrase grouping, and characteristic ending. Decide which features must survive a transformation. A recurring seed guarantees reproducibility; it does not guarantee a recognizable tune.

Develop a phrase by changing one or two dimensions at a time: transpose it, lengthen its durations, fragment its opening, move it to another instrument, or place it over new harmony. Inversion and extensive rhythmic change are stronger transformations and need listening checks. Keep a clear opening statement available so listeners have an identity to learn before hearing fragments.

### Harmony and emotional ambiguity

Separate a chord's local color from its role in a phrase. A stable bass pedal can hold a scene open while upper voices change. Common tones and small voice movements can connect arrangements; a cadence can mark an arrival or deliberately postpone one. Test a familiar melody over alternate bass notes and chord qualities, and identify where the altered harmony makes its important notes sound stable or tense.

Do not encode major as happy and minor as sad. Register, pacing, performance, dissonance, rhythmic density, and narrative context can change an interpretation. Write down the intended function of a passage—waiting, searching, accepting, resisting—before choosing harmonic devices. Use the existing [theory and sound study](music-theory-and-sound.md) for our implemented tonal heuristics and its separately licensed textbook references.

### Rhythm and variable meter for the math rock direction

Distinguish meter, accent grouping, syncopation, and polyrhythm. A 7/8 bar grouped 2+2+3 differs from the same duration grouped 3+2+2. Syncopation can create surprise within a stable meter. A true polyrhythm involves contrasting simultaneous subdivisions; merely changing bar lengths is not a polyrhythm.

For an original Way exercise, use eighth-note units: three two-unit gestures followed by a one-unit rest fill a seven-unit bar. Repeat that phrase in 7/8, then retain its first seven units in 4/4 and add one unit of space. Keep the pitches and opening rhythm stable while testing the change in phrase ending. This is an authored experiment, not a transcription of a reference score.

Allow a stable pulse or recurring accent pattern to orient listeners while another part shifts. Changes should have a dramatic purpose, such as playful imbalance becoming controlled propulsion. Avoid changing meter just because telemetry changed. Odd meter does not itself signify danger or sophistication.

### Form and transitions

Compose entrances, continuations, and exits alongside the loops. Track phrase position and the expected arrival, not only the current bar. Waiting for a bar boundary avoids some timing problems, but it cannot resolve an incompatible bass note or an unfinished melody.

Horizontal resequencing chooses successive sections; vertical layering changes simultaneous parts. Short event cues and transition passages have different jobs. [Audiokinetic's interactive music documentation](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/public-library/2024.1.8_8893/?id=creating_interactive_music&source=Help) explicitly separates these structures and transition controls. These are architecture references, not a proposed Wwise dependency. The Nintendo and Halo cases demonstrate why the content must be written for those choices.

Give immediate events a suitable response while allowing slower musical development to finish. An attack sound can acknowledge a hit immediately; the score may build over the next phrase. Important story cues can justify interruption. Author the return from an interruption too.

### Orchestration and silence

Choose which voice the listener should follow. Let the other instruments support, answer, or remain absent. Register, articulation, breathing room, and realistic phrase lengths matter as much as instrument selection. Do not use randomized timing or velocity as a substitute for a performed phrase.

Treat musical silence separately from muting the whole game. Dialogue, environmental sound, and player attention can remain active. Specify why music stops, how long absence can last, and what makes its return appropriate. Distinguish silence, a held texture, and an unresolved musical pause during evaluation.

## Apply the research to Way's proposed dramatic arc

These are composition hypotheses to audition, not automatic emotion rules.

| Game moment | What the music should communicate | Variation to study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | A clear identity the player can learn | Complete theme, distinctive rhythm, uncluttered accompaniment. |
| Contemplative opening | Vulnerability and an unfinished journey | More space, restrained register, delayed phrase closure. |
| First player control | Curiosity and growing agency | Retain the theme; add answering gestures and a light, asymmetric groove. |
| Movement and ordinary actions | Participation without musical chatter | Change accompaniment over phrases; reserve foreground responses for meaningful actions. |
| Danger | Pressure with a connection to the same world | Compress or fragment the theme, strengthen pulse, postpone harmonic release. |
| Revelation or loss | A changed understanding of something familiar | Reharmonize or expose a learned fragment; allow a pause. |
| Resolution | Arrival earned by what came before | Recover a fuller statement and a deliberate ending; compare quiet and large-scale versions. |

This progression borrows design questions from the references, not their melodies or recognizable signature arrangements. The theme remains authored and stable across game restarts. Procedural variation should develop that theme within constraints the composer has chosen.

## Current readiness and research priorities

Inspection of version 0.5 confirms that we have enough infrastructure to run these studies: recorded instruments, a fixed eight-degree theme, multiple arrangements, variable meter, a portable director, movement/action responses, and timed story cues. The research bottleneck is now musical content and evaluation.

The present theme interface stores eight scale degrees, with note timing supplied by arrangement rules. It cannot yet author a complete rhythmic identity independently of those rules. The director also uses general action activity and preset tempo/intensity targets; it does not infer the narrative meaning of an action. Phrase boundaries and smoothing exist, but dedicated harmonic bridges, a deliberate silence policy, and richer thematic development remain gaps. These observations come from [composer.mjs](../src/composer.mjs) and [director.mjs](../src/director.mjs), not from the soundtrack sources.

The first task is to learn which musical choices work. Extending the schema or replacing a renderer before that evidence would add machinery without answering the composition question.

The subsequent authoring requirement is that the user supplies melodies, countermelodies, and signature hooks. Theme exercises below concern composer-authored material; they are not a request to automatically generate those identities. The [visual instrument and keyboard authoring study](visual-instruments-and-keyboard-authoring.md) describes how performance input and instrument design can support that direction.

## Listening and composition study plan

### Compare actual music in its game context

Use official releases or lawful game playback. For each pair, record the recognizable feature, the changing feature, phrase lengths, texture, entrance/exit behavior, and the scene's meaning. Do not assume that album ordering reproduces the live score.

| Listening assignment | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Mario Ground Theme and its alternate sections | What distinguishes each idea while keeping the whole coherent? |
| Ocarina of Time Hyrule Field during travel and enemy approach | Which changes preserve continuity, and where are the joins? |
| Final Fantasy VII Aeris's Theme in different narrative contexts; X To Zanarkand in its opening | How much of the emotional association depends on story knowledge? |
| Halo theme and gameplay passages with and without music | What does an entrance accomplish after an unscored interval? |
| Minecraft Wet Hands and Subwoofer Lullaby, then ordinary play | Does an apparently incidental entrance gain meaning through the player's activity? |
| Undertale Once Upon a Time and Undertale | Which relationships can we confirm by ear, and which require careful transcription? |
| Celeste First Steps and Celestial Resort gameplay | How do identity and dramatic instability differ? |
| DOOM BFG Division and Rip & Tear | What carries recognition: rhythm, bass movement, production, or melody? |
| Journey Nascence and Apotheosis | What remains identifiable across the dramatic change? |
| Rapture The Mourning Tree and I Hope You Find Peace | How do voice and scene context support different human stories? |
| The Last of Us main theme and gameplay around dialogue | How much musical space is needed? |
| Silent Hill 2 Theme of Laura and Promise | What creates vulnerability or unease without a generic horror gesture? |

These are assignments for close listening, not assertions that every paired track shares one motif. Log observations separately from interpretations. Later expansion should include Chrono Trigger, Skyrim, Kingdom Hearts, and examples outside Western tonal traditions before generalizing this corpus.

### Compose small controlled studies with the tools we have

1. **Theme study:** Write three original candidate themes with explicit rhythm and rests. Present each on one plain instrument before orchestration. Test what listeners can sing, tap, or recognize after a delay.
2. **Transformation study:** Choose a candidate and make title, contemplative, playful, danger, and resolution versions. Hold its defining features steady. Ask whether listeners identify the same theme and whether the intended differences are perceived.
3. **Meter study:** Compare stable 4/4, stable grouped 7/8, and a planned meter cycle using the same theme. Separate the effects of tempo, density, and accents from bar length. Record orientation, interest, and fatigue.
4. **Narrative study:** Score the same short gameplay sequence with restrained changes, frequent responses, and planned silence. Include lingering, backtracking, interruptions, and repeated battles. Ask where music supports agency or becomes distracting.
5. **Transition study:** Compare an ordinary bar switch with an authored exit and entry. Listen specifically for unfinished phrases, conflicting harmony, and whether delayed changes still feel responsive.

Keep instrument bank and approximate perceived loudness consistent when comparing composition. Counterbalance presentation order and record prior familiarity, musical background, and headphone/speaker setup. Use unfamiliar comparison fragments to detect guesses in recognition tasks. Separate immediate preference, delayed recognition, perceived narrative fit, and distraction; they are different outcomes. A small exploratory listening group can guide iteration but cannot establish a universal formula.

The deliverable from this phase should be an authored theme, a set of listener-tested transformations, a short dramatic scoring brief, and a log of listener observations. Code changes should follow the specific limitations those studies expose.
